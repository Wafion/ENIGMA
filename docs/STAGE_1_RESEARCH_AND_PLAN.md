## 1. HISTORICAL & DOCUMENTARY RESEARCH NOTES

### Epistemological Framework: Fact vs. Fiction Separation
A core requirement of ENIGMA's editorial integrity is maintaining an explicit, unmistakable boundary between:
- **Layer A — The Fictional Mythology (`IN-UNIVERSE MYTHOLOGY` / `RECONSTRUCTED FICTIONAL MATERIAL`)**: The 200-year folkloric timeline invented between 1993 and 1999 by filmmakers Daniel Myrick, Eduardo Sánchez, and Ben Rock / Gregg Hale (Haxan Films) for *The Blair Witch Project* (1999), the original *BlairWitch.com* website, and the Sci-Fi Channel mockumentary *Curse of the Blair Witch* (aired July 11, 1999).
- **Layer B — The Real-World Phenomenon (`GENUINE HISTORICAL MATERIAL` / `GENUINE PRODUCTION RECORD` / `MODERN CONTEXTUAL PHOTOGRAPHY`)**: The actual history of Burkittsville, Frederick County, Maryland; the October 1997 eight-day guerrilla shoot in Seneca Creek State Park, Burkittsville, and Patapsco Valley State Park; the revolutionary 1998–1999 internet marketing campaign; and the cultural watershed that redefined independent cinema.

### Layer A: The Fictional Mythology (In-Universe Lore)
*All events below are fictional narrative constructs created by Haxan Films and must be labeled `[IN-UNIVERSE FICTION / RECONSTRUCTED LORE]` throughout the interface.*

1. **February 1785 — The Banishment of Elly Kedward**:
   - In the fictional colonial settlement of **Blair, Maryland** (which the mythology places on the site of modern-day Burkittsville), several children accuse an Irish immigrant woman named **Elly Kedward** of luring them into her home to draw blood from them.
   - Found guilty of witchcraft by the township magistrate, Kedward is bound to a wooden cart, driven into the sub-zero depths of the Black Hills forest during a harsh winter, and abandoned to die of exposure.
2. **Winter 1786 — The Abandonment of Blair**:
   - By mid-winter 1786, all of Elly Kedward's young accusers—along with half of the town's children—vanish without physical trace. Fearing a seasonal curse tied to the surrounding woods, the surviving residents flee Blair and swear an oath never to speak Elly Kedward's name again.
3. **1809 — *The Blair Witch Cult* Pamphlet**:
   - A rare, anonymous woodcut-illustrated book titled *The Blair Witch Cult* is purportedly published in 1809, recounting the curse of the banished witch and strange stick-effigy markings in the timberlands. (In reality, this artifact was fabricated by production designer Ben Rock and the Haxan team for the 1999 *Curse of the Blair Witch* broadcast and *BlairWitch.com* archive).
4. **1820 / 1824 — Founding of Burkittsville**:
   - Within the lore, Henry Burkitt purchases the abandoned settlement of Blair and re-establishes it as Burkittsville in 1824. *(Note: In real history, Burkittsville was founded in 1824 by Henry Burkitt and Joshua Harley, but it was never previously named "Blair" and had no colonial witch trials.)*
5. **August 1825 — Eileen Treacle at Tappy East Creek**:
   - Eleven witnesses claim to see a pale woman's hand reach out of the shallow water of **Tappy East Creek** and pull ten-year-old **Eileen Treacle** beneath the surface. Her body is never recovered, and for thirteen days afterward, the creek becomes clogged with oily bundles of woven sticks, rendering the water undrinkable for cattle.
6. **March 1886 — Robin Weaver & The Coffin Rock Massacre**:
   - Eight-year-old **Robin Weaver** wanders into the Black Hills at dusk. While search parties comb the woods, Weaver walks back into town three days later, describing an old woman whose feet "never touched the forest floor."
   - One five-man search party led by Turlock fails to return. Weeks later, a second search team finds the five men at **Coffin Rock**, bound together at the wrists and ankles in a pentagram formation and ritually disemboweled, with mysterious runic symbols carved into their faces and hands. When the searchers return hours later with the county sheriff and photographer, the corpses have vanished without a trace, though the lingering odor of decay and blood on the rock remain.
7. **November 1940 – May 1941 — Rustin Parr & The Burkittsville Seven**:
   - Beginning in November 1940, seven children (starting with Emily Hollands) are abducted from the Burkittsville area.
   - On **May 25, 1941**, a reclusive woodsman named **Rustin Parr**, who lived in a remote cabin four miles into the Black Hills, walks into a local market in Burkittsville and tells the shopkeeper: *"I'm finally finished."*
   - Police hike four hours to Parr's woodland house and uncover seven shallow graves in the dirt cellar. Parr confesses that an spectral old woman in a hooded cloak had been whispering to him in the woods for months, instructing him to bring the children into his basement in pairs—forcing one child to stand facing the corner of the cellar listening while he murdered the other, because he could not bear the way their eyes looked at him. Parr is convicted and hanged on November 22, 1941, and the town burns his house to the ground.
8. **October 20–28, 1994 — The Montgomery College Student Disappearance**:
   - Three student filmmakers—director **Heather Donahue**, sound recordist **Michael C. Williams**, and cinematographer **Joshua Leonard**—travel to Burkittsville in October 1994 to shoot a weekend student documentary about the Blair Witch legend.
   - After interviewing locals in town on October 20 and visiting Burkittsville Cemetery and Coffin Rock on October 21, they hike into the Black Hills Forest and become hopelessly disoriented. Over five terrifying nights, they encounter cairns of seven stones around their tent, dozens of humanoid stick figures (*twana*) suspended from the trees, nocturnal acoustic anomalies, and Joshua's sudden disappearance.
   - On October 16, 1995—nearly one year after an extensive ten-day Maryland State Police search is called off—students from the University of Maryland anthropology department excavate a canvas duffel bag buried beneath the 100-year-old foundation of a burned cabin (Westerfield cabin), containing 11 rolls of black-and-white 16mm film, Hi8 video cassettes, DAT audio tapes, and Heather Donahue's journal.

### Layer B: The Real-World History & Production Facts
*All events below are verified historical and production facts and must be labeled `[VERIFIED HISTORICAL / PRODUCTION RECORD]`.*

1. **Real Burkittsville, Maryland**:
   - Located in southwestern **Frederick County, Maryland**, at the eastern base of **South Mountain** (specifically below **Crampton's Gap**).
   - Settled in the mid-18th century by German and Swiss-German farmers; laid out in 1824 by Joshua Harley (western section, "Harley's Post Office") and Major Henry Burkitt (eastern section), consolidated as **Burkittsville** around 1829.
   - Site of the real **Battle of Crampton's Gap** (September 14, 1862) during the Maryland Campaign of the American Civil War, where Union forces under Maj. Gen. William B. Franklin stormed Confederate positions along the stone walls at the mountain base, turning Burkittsville's churches (including Resurrection Reformed Church and St. Paul's Lutheran Church) into field hospitals.
   - Listed in its entirety on the National Register of Historic Places in 1975 as the **Burkittsville Historic District**.
2. **Conception & Method Production (1993–1997)**:
   - Conceived in 1993 by University of Central Florida film students **Daniel Myrick** and **Eduardo Sánchez**, alongside producers **Gregg Hale**, **Robin Cowie**, and **Michael Monello** (forming **Haxan Films**).
   - Drawing inspiration from 1970s pseudodocumentaries like *In Search of...*, *The Legend of Boggy Creek* (1972), and *Cannibal Holocaust* (1980), they drafted a 35-page outline in which all dialogue would be improvised.
   - Cast three unknown actors—**Heather Donahue**, **Joshua Leonard**, and **Michael C. Williams**—who used their real legal names in the film.
   - **Principal Photography (October 23–31, 1997)**: Shot over 8 consecutive days. Only the opening town interviews and cemetery scenes were filmed in actual Burkittsville; the forest sequences were filmed primarily in **Seneca Creek State Park** (Montgomery County, MD, including the ruins of **Black Rock Mill**), and the climax inside the ruined house was filmed at the historic **Griggs House** (built c. 1850) in **Granite, Maryland**, inside **Patapsco Valley State Park**.
   - **Method Directing & Tactical GPS Tracking**: Leonard operated a **CP-16 16mm film camera** (black-and-white documentary footage) while Donahue operated a consumer **RCA Hi8 camcorder** (color behind-the-scenes footage), with Williams operating a **Tascam DAT recorder**. The directors guided the actors via a Garmin GPS unit to drop-boxes (milk crates marked with Bicycle Safety Flags) containing daily psychological "directing notes" in 35mm film canisters alongside progressively reduced food rations. At night, the crew ambushed the campsite with boombox-amplified recordings of children laughing, snapped deadwood branches, shook the tent, and hung woven stick figures in the surrounding oaks.
3. **The 1998–1999 Digital Blur & Cultural Phenomenon**:
   - In June 1998, Haxan Films launched **BlairWitch.com**, presenting the 1785–1994 timeline, fabricated police evidence photos, Heather's journal pages, and chronological reels as a factual case archive. IMDb initially listed Heather Donahue, Joshua Leonard, and Michael C. Williams as *"Missing, presumed dead"*.
   - Premiered at the **Sundance Film Festival** at midnight on January 23, 1999; acquired by **Artisan Entertainment** for \$1.1 million.
   - Released nationwide in July 1999 after the Sci-Fi Channel aired *Curse of the Blair Witch*, grossing **\$248.6 million worldwide** on an original production budget of \$35,000–\$60,000 (approximately \$200,000–\$500,000 post-acquisition), making it one of the most profitable independent films in cinema history.
   - Real-world impact on Burkittsville (~150 residents): The quiet historic village was inundated by thousands of tourists, leading to the repeated theft of the town's wooden welcome signs, vandalism at the historic Burkittsville Cemetery, and the eventual demolition of the historic **Griggs House** in Patapsco Valley State Park in December 1999.

## 4. TECHNICAL IMPLEMENTATION PLAN (ASCII & STORY SYSTEM)

### 4.1 Study of the ASCII References
We studied the four references specified in the prompt:
1. **ASCII Magic (`https://www.ascii-magic.com/ascii`)**: Demonstrates multi-palette luminance-to-character mapping, custom character density ramps, threshold slicing, Sobel edge-direction awareness, color/monochrome modes, and CRT/halftone post-processing.
2. **Ascii-Motion (`https://www.ascii-motion.com/` & `https://github.com/cameronfoxly/Ascii-Motion`)**: Demonstrates grid-based cell buffers, real-time Canvas 2D character cell rendering (`fillText` with pre-computed character glyph atlases or batched row rendering), keyframe/timeline transitions, and multi-layer compositing without polluting the DOM with thousands of `<span>` tags.
3. **ASCIIGen (`https://asciigen.art/#library`)**: Demonstrates responsive player controls, frame-rate capping (`requestAnimationFrame` throttled to 24–30fps for cinematic texture), and visibility-aware pausing via `IntersectionObserver`.

### 4.2 ENIGMA Canvas 2D ASCII Engine Architecture
To ensure 60fps scroll performance and low CPU overhead on ordinary laptops and mobile devices:
- **Offscreen Image Sampling**: Source images (or procedural video frames) are drawn once to a lightweight offscreen `<canvas>` scaled to the target grid dimensions (`cols × rows`, typically `80×45` to `140×80` depending on viewport width and user-selected density).
- **Luminance + Edge + Dither Pipeline**:
  - Computes perceptual luminance $Y = 0.299R + 0.587G + 0.114B$, applying contrast, brightness, gamma, and threshold controls.

- **Four Distinct Storytelling ASCII Modes**:
  1. **Prologue Emergent Canopy (`ASCIICanopyHero`)**: Scroll-driven emergence from sparse nocturnal noise into the high-resolution Seneca Creek forest photograph, with optional cursor/touch flashlight reveal.
  2. **Scroll-Driven Photographic Revelation (`ASCIIImage` with Interactive Inspector)**: Every archival photograph can be viewed in its original high-resolution state, scrubbed with an interactive split-screen Optical/ASCII Loupe, or live-adjusted with the user's own ASCII Studio drawer (Palette, Density, Contrast, Brightness, Invert, Color Mode).

  - Respects `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` (Plus an explicit in-app **"Reduced Motion / Static Archive"** toggle in the top bar).
  - Keeps the underlying semantic `<img>` and `<figcaption>` accessible to screen readers.
